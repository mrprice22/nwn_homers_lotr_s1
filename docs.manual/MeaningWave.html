MeaningWave: A Guide to the Guides

"Even here, in the Third Age, we hear the signal. The Wave does not respect the boundaries between worlds."

This is a fan page — not affiliated with or endorsed by Akira the Don or Meaningwave. It is a player's guide to the Meaningwave content woven into Homer's LOTR, a Neverwinter Nights module, where the Wave Lord's signal has somehow bled through the Veil into Middle-earth. Seven philosopher-legends, summoned by Akira's mixtape, now walk the roads of Arda and wait to be found.

To hear the real music, support Akira, and explore the full catalogue of philosopher-music from the source:

▶ meaningwave.com — official home of the Wave Lord, Akira the Don

Seven legends have been pulled across worlds into Middle-earth by Akira the Don's mixtape. Find them, prove yourself worthy, and they will walk with you in dream and in deed.

This guide lists where each legend stands, the shortest route from the Well of Eru to find them, and how each is unlocked. Once you have unlocked at least one, the Wayshrine of the Wave in the Well of Eru opens the door to the Hall of Legends — Akira's hub.

How unlocking works. Each guide keeps a bank of 20 questions and asks you five, drawn at random — with the answer options shuffled every time, so there is no fixed pattern to memorise. Answer at least four of the five correctly and the guide will recognise you. Fall short and you can simply try again: a fresh five are drawn. Once unlocked they appear in the rest-time / emote-wand menu under "[Summon a Legend.]" — pick them to bring them along as a henchman for the journey.

Study up. Because the questions now span each legend's whole body of work, the "Further reading" and "On Meaningwave" links under every guide below are your best prep — all are free and online (Wikipedia, the figures' own sites, and Akira the Don's tracks).

Tip. Resting (or activating the emote wand item) opens the legacy emote menu. A new option, "[Summon a Legend.]", appears at the bottom once you have at least one guide. Only the legends you have unlocked appear in the submenu.

Practice here. Each legend below has a practice quiz right on this page — the same five-of-twenty draw and 4-of-5 pass mark as in-game, so you can rehearse before you make the trip. It only reports your score; it does not unlock anything in the module.

The Hall of Legends

Location
The Hall of Legends — a hub area reached only via the Wayshrine.
Unlock
Unlock any one of the seven legends first.

Path from Well of Eru (1 hop)

  1. Well of Eru → The Hall of Legends: touch the Wayshrine of the Wave, the black obelisk in the Well of Eru.

Inside the Hall, Akira the Don stands at the centre, surrounded by seven pedestals. Each pedestal that still holds a stone statue is a legend you have yet to unlock; pedestals whose statue you have already earned now show the live figure, who will greet you with a short post-unlock benediction.

Final reward — Akira's Mixtape. When all seven legends are yours, return to the Hall and ask Akira for the mixtape. The ring can optionally be consumed to permanently grant +1 to all ability scores.

Combat Styles

Once a guide is walking with you as a henchman, you can tell them how to fight. Talk to them and choose "Let's talk tactics." — the option only appears while they are summoned. Each guide remembers their style until you change it or they are dismissed.

Tip: Set Peterson to Aggressive going into a boss fight — he will open with an epic nuke. Swap Watts to Guardian if you are taking heavy damage and need a dedicated healer. Jung in Shadow mode is most effective when you are tanking: his sneak attacks land consistently against foes already focused on you. Jocko in Extreme Ownership keeps heat off you in a crowd — put him on it and let him handle the pile-on.

Counterspell (caster mode)

The five spellcasting legends — Jordan Peterson, Alan Watts, Joseph Campbell, Terence McKenna, and Marcus Aurelius — have a fourth tactical option: "Counter the enemy's magic." In this mode the guide stops nuking or healing and instead readies counterspells against the nearest enemy spellcaster, tearing apart their magic as it is cast. A guide's Spellcraft is greatly sharpened while they walk with you, so the counters land reliably. If no enemy mage is in sight they fall back to their normal combat behaviour, so the mode is safe to leave on against mixed groups.

Gear & Growth

Every legend who walks with you wears the Aegis of Meaning, a bound ring that cannot be removed, looted, or sold. It grants 15/- damage resistance and 25% immunity against every type of damage — bludgeoning, piercing and slashing, plus acid, cold, fire, electrical, magical, divine, positive, negative, and sonic — making the guides far more durable in the late game.

The legends also grow stronger the more of them you have awakened. Each time you summon a guide they are empowered according to how many of the seven you have unlocked:

  • +6 to every ability score for each legend unlocked — so the full roster of seven grants +42 to all stats.
  • +2 divine and +2 positive weapon damage for each legend unlocked, applied to their weapon — or, for the unarmed monks Watts and Jocko, to their gloves and bracers.

The growth is recalculated every time a guide is summoned, so unlocking another legend immediately strengthens whoever you bring along next. (Each weapon damage type is capped at +20.)

Battlefield Cures

Some legends carry emergency restorative magic that they cast automatically to save the party — no matter which combat style you have set, and whether or not they are in combat:

  • Stone to Flesh — if a party member is turned to stone, Peterson, Watts, or McKenna will restore them. 3 uses, refreshed by resting.
  • Restoration — if a party member suffers ability-score damage, Peterson, Watts, McKenna, or Aurelius will cure it. 3 uses, refreshed by resting.

Only one legend walks with you at a time, so keep a caster summoned if you expect basilisks, medusas, cockatrices, or ability-draining foes.
